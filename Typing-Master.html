Finger Placement & Posture Guide

Touch typing means typing without looking at the keyboard. This is only possible if every finger always returns to its home position.

The Home Row — Your Base Position

Place both hands on the middle row. Rest your left index finger on F and your right index finger on J. You'll feel a small raised bump on both keys — that's your anchor. Every other finger finds its key from here.

Correct Posture Checklist

  • Sit up straight — back supported by the chair
  • Feet flat on the floor, knees at 90°
  • Elbows bent at roughly 90°, close to your sides
  • Wrists straight — not bent up or down
  • Screen at eye level, arm's length away
  • Fingers curved over home row keys
  • Do not rest wrists on the desk while typing
  • Look at the screen, not the keyboard

How to Build Speed

  • Accuracy first, speed second. 100% accurate typing naturally speeds up.
  • Practice 15–20 minutes daily. Short sessions beat marathons.
  • Never look down. If you forget a key, glance once and remember.
  • Don't stop to fix every typo mid-word — finish, then correct.
  • Use the correct finger even if it feels slow. Wrong habits are hard to unlearn.
  • Track your WPM weekly. Improvements come in steps.

Understanding Your Stats

  • WPM: Words per minute. 1 word = 5 characters.
  • Accuracy: Percentage of characters typed correctly.
  • Slow (0–25): Beginner. Focus on accuracy.
  • Average (25–45): Casual typist. Most people start here.
  • Fluent (45–65): Comfortable speed for work.
  • Fast (65–85): Very good. Above most office workers.
  • Expert (85+): Professional level.
